# Practice guide

Open the [practice website](https://mbianchidev.github.io/engineering-interviews/) in a browser with JavaScript enabled. No account or installation is required. Questions intentionally have no answer keys, automated scoring, or code runner.

## Browse and write answers

Select **Browse Topics**, choose a topic, and expand a question. Write in **Your Response** and leave the text box to save. Return to the same question in the same browser to revisit or edit the answer. **Clear Response** removes that answer.

The topics cover engineering fundamentals, software design, coding and problem solving, observability, SRE, and platform engineering. Coding prompts use reusable scenarios rather than reproducing company take-home assignments.

## Practice a round

Select **Start Practicing** and choose a mode:

| Mode | Timer | Rollover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Easy | No time limit | None |
| Standard | Five minutes per question | Unused time is available to later questions in the round |
| Hard | Five minutes per question | None |

Leave topics unselected for the full bank, or select categories and subtopics with at least ten questions in total. Start the round, write a response, and select **Next Question** or **Skip** to continue. Timed modes advance automatically when time expires.

After ten questions, rate your confidence, effectiveness, and knowledge from 1 to 5. All three ratings are required to submit the self-evaluation. Past evaluations can be expanded from the practice setup screen. These are your own ratings, not an assessment of answer correctness.

## Questions to ask companies

Use **Questions to Ask Companies** to explore culture, growth, role expectations, work-life balance, and company fit. Expand individual sections or use **Expand All**. Pick a few relevant questions rather than treating the list as a mandatory script.

## Saved work and resetting

Responses save when the text box loses focus or the practice question changes. Viewed-question IDs and self-evaluations also stay in browser local storage. Revisit saved practice responses through Browse Topics.

There is no backend, account, cross-device sync, or backup/export feature. Local and hosted sites use different origins and do not share stored work. An active round and its timer are not persisted across page reloads.

**Clear All Responses** on the practice setup screen removes saved answers and viewed-question progress after confirmation. **Clear All Evaluations** removes the evaluation history separately. These actions cannot be undone. Clearing site data in your browser removes all saved work; copy any responses you want to keep first.

## Troubleshooting

- If responses disappear after closing a private window, use a normal browser profile with local storage available. Different profiles and devices have separate saved work.
- If a filtered round cannot start, select subtopics containing at least ten questions, or clear the filter.
- If a timer resets after a reload, start a new round. Only responses, viewed-question history, and submitted evaluations are saved.
- If the website is blank, check that JavaScript is enabled. For local setup or static-host route issues, see the [developer guide](../SETUP.md).

For reproducible non-sensitive bugs, use the repository's [issues](https://github.com/mbianchidev/engineering-interviews/issues). Do not include private interview responses or credentials in reports.

## STAR method

S.T.A.R. is a useful acronym and an effective formula for structuring your interview responses for tech companies.

- **Situation** (20%), explain the situation so that your interviewer understands the context of your example, they do not need to know every technical detail.
- **Task** (10%), talk about the task that you took responsibility for completing or the goal of your efforts.
- **Action** (60%), describe the actions that you personally took to complete the task or reach the end goal. Highlight skills or character traits addressed in the question. Use a lot of "I" instead of "we".
- **Result** (10%), explain the positive outcomes or results generated by your actions or efforts. Here, it is important to highlight quantifiable results. You may also want to emphasize what you learned from the experience or your key takeaways. This can even be a not-so-pleasant experience, in that case still try to focus on positive takeaways.
